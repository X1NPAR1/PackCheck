Minecraft Port Forwarding: Let Friends Join

If friends on the same Wi-Fi can join but people outside cannot, the router is dropping their connection. You fix it once by opening the server port in the Windows firewall and forwarding it on the router.

Step by step

  1. Open the port in Windows. On PackCheck's Online page press Open to the internet. It adds a firewall rule for the server port after one administrator prompt.
  2. Let PackCheck try UPnP. Many routers accept automatic forwarding (UPnP). If yours does, you are done.
  3. Forward the port by hand if needed. Open your router's page (usually http://192.168.1.1/), find Port Forwarding, NAT or Virtual Server, and add a rule: TCP, external port 25565, internal IP of your PC, internal port 25565.
  4. Give your PC a fixed local IP. Reserve the PC's address in the router's DHCP settings so the rule keeps working after a restart.
  5. Test from outside. Press Test from outside in PackCheck. Green means friends can join with your public IP and port.

Still not reachable?

  • CGNAT: if the WAN IP on your router page differs from your public IP (or starts with 100.64–100.127), your provider shares one IP between customers. Ask for a public IP or use a tunnel service.
  • Two routers: forward on both, or put the provider's modem in bridge mode.
  • Testing from home: many routers cannot connect to their own public IP (no NAT loopback). Use the local address at home.
  • Changing IP: share a free DuckDNS name instead of the raw IP; PackCheck can keep it updated.

Questions

What port does Minecraft Java use?

25565 over TCP by default. PackCheck picks a free port automatically and shows it on the Online page.

Is port forwarding safe?

It only opens the Minecraft port. Keep online mode and a whitelist on, and close the port again from the Online page when you do not need it.

Why can't my friends join with my IP?

Usually the router is not forwarding the port, the Windows firewall blocks it, or your provider uses CGNAT.
